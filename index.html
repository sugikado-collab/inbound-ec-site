The difference

This is not a rubber stamp.

The word gets translated as “Japanese stamp,” and the translation destroys it. A stamp repeats. A seal declares. One is a tool for paperwork; the other is a legal instrument and an inheritance.

01

The script cannot be read

Seals are carved in tensho, a script two thousand years old. Even Japanese people rarely read it at a glance. That is the point — a mark that is hard to copy, and harder to forge.

02

One person, one seal

A registered seal — jitsuin — may be held by only one person, and only one may be registered. No duplicate is permitted to exist.

03

Given at a threshold

A seal is traditionally given when a life changes: coming of age, marriage, the founding of a business. It is not bought casually. It is given.

The instant it is pressed, there is no undoing it.

The making

Four weeks, one hand.

  1. 1

    The name is set

    Your name is laid out in tensho by a carver, balanced to the circle. No two layouts are the same.

  2. 2

    The face is cut

    The face is cut into the horn or wood with a hand chisel. The line is never perfectly straight, and it is not meant to be.

  3. 3

    The impression is proofed

    The seal is pressed, examined under light, and returned to the chisel until the red reads clean.

  4. 4

    It is boxed and sent

    Wrapped in paulownia with a pad of vermillion paste, and shipped from Japan.
